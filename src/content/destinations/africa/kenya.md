Kenya is East Africa's hub: the busiest airport in the region, a large tech and NGO community in Nairobi, and the safari destination most people picture when they think of Africa.

## Nairobi

The capital is where you land and, for many, where they end up staying. Westlands, Kilimani and Karen have good apartments, coworkings and cafés. Traffic is severe; choose where you live based on where you need to be.

## Safari without overpaying

- **Masai Mara**: the big one, especially during the wildebeest migration between July and October. Prices peak then.
- **Amboseli**: elephants with Kilimanjaro in the background, a shorter drive from Nairobi.
- **Nairobi National Park**: a real park inside city limits, a half-day option.
- **Budget approach**: group tours from Nairobi start around 150 USD per day including transport, park fees and camping. Lodges run from 300 to over 1,000 USD per night.

## The coast

Diani Beach, south of Mombasa, is the most popular stretch: white sand, kitesurfing and a growing remote-work community. Lamu is quieter and more historic.

## Connectivity

Safaricom has excellent coverage and the M-Pesa mobile money system is used for almost everything; get a Safaricom SIM on arrival and register for M-Pesa if you stay more than a few days.

## Practical notes

Apply for the eTA online at least a few days before travel. Yellow fever vaccination may be required depending on where you are arriving from. Malaria prophylaxis is advised for the coast and low-lying parks, not usually for Nairobi.
