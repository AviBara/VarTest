A safari can cost 150 USD a day or 1,500. The wildlife is the same. The difference is where you sleep, how you get there and whether you booked through a chain of intermediaries.

## The three cost blocks

1. **Park fees**: fixed per person per day and set by the park. Typically 50 to 100 USD in Kenya and Tanzania, less in South Africa and Namibia. No way to avoid these.
2. **Transport**: a 4x4 with driver-guide is the big variable in East Africa. Shared group tours split it; private vehicles multiply it.
3. **Lodging**: camping inside a park from 20 USD; mid-range tented camps 150 to 300; luxury lodges 500 and up, usually with meals and game drives included.

## Three ways to halve the price

- **Self-drive in Southern Africa**: South Africa's Kruger and Namibia's Etosha are designed for it. Hire a car, book park accommodation directly, buy food in supermarkets.
- **Join a group tour from the city**: in Nairobi or Arusha, operators run scheduled departures. Camping tours with a cook and a guide start around 150 USD per day all-in.
- **Travel in shoulder season**: park fees stay the same, but lodges drop rates by 30 to 50 percent outside migration months and holidays.

## What to check before paying a deposit

- Are park fees included or extra?
- Is the vehicle a closed minivan or an open-sided 4x4? It matters for photography and comfort.
- How many people per vehicle? Six or fewer keeps everyone at a window.
- Does the operator have a physical office and reviews you can verify?

## Insurance

Evacuation cover is not optional on safari; the nearest hospital can be hours away. Confirm your policy covers medical evacuation by air.
