Africa flips the usual travel-connectivity advice. Regional eSIMs exist but are expensive per gigabyte, while local SIMs are among the cheapest in the world and unlock mobile money, which you will want anyway.

## The general rule

- **Arrive with a small eSIM** (1 to 3 GB) so you have data at the airport for a taxi and a map.
- **Buy a local SIM within a day or two**. Registration with your passport is required in most countries and takes a few minutes at an official shop.

## Country notes

| Country | Best operator | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| South Africa | Vodacom, MTN | Good 4G/5G in cities; eSIM works fine as a backup |
| Kenya | Safaricom | Register for M-Pesa; used for taxis, shops, even market stalls |
| Morocco | Maroc Telecom | Widest coverage in the desert and mountains |
| Tanzania | Vodacom, Airtel | Strong along tourist routes; patchy in the parks |
| Egypt | Vodafone | Tourist SIM packages at the airport are convenient |
| Namibia | MTC | Coverage drops fast outside towns; download maps |

## Mobile money

In East Africa, phone-based payments have replaced cash for many transactions. Setting up M-Pesa (Kenya, Tanzania) or MTN Mobile Money (Ghana, Uganda, Rwanda) takes a passport and a few minutes and makes daily life much easier.

## Power

Load shedding in South Africa and irregular power elsewhere make a power bank essential. Pick accommodation with a generator or solar backup if you have deadlines.
