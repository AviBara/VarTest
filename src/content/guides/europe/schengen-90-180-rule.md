If you enter Europe on a passport that does not need a visa, you can spend 90 days in the Schengen area within any rolling 180-day window. The rule is simple to state and surprisingly easy to get wrong.

## How the count works

- The window is **rolling**: on any given day, look back 180 days and count the days you were inside Schengen. That number must not exceed 90.
- Entry day and exit day both count as full days.
- Days in non-Schengen European countries (the UK, Ireland, Georgia, Albania, most of the Balkans, Türkiye) do not count.

## Tracking it

Use the official EU short-stay calculator or any of the common tracker apps. Keep entry and exit stamps readable, and expect border checks to become automated with the Entry/Exit System.

## Staying longer legally

1. **Alternate with non-Schengen countries**: 90 days in Schengen, then time in Georgia, the Balkans or the UK, then back.
2. **National long-stay visas**: digital nomad visas (Spain, Portugal, Croatia, Estonia and others) let you stay in that country beyond 90 days and usually still travel within Schengen.
3. **Bilateral agreements**: a few countries have older agreements that allow extra days for specific passports. Rare and inconsistently applied; do not rely on them without confirmation.

## Overstaying

Even one day over can lead to a fine and an entry ban. Border officers see your full history once the automated system is fully rolled out, so plan around the rule rather than hoping for leniency.
